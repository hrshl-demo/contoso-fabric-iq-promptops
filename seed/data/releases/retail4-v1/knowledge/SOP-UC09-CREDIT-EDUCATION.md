## Purpose
This procedure guides staff on correcting misunderstandings about credit and BNPL in a factual, educational manner. The focus is to clarify what the customer believes, verify the actual terms of any existing commitment, and avoid moving into product discussion where the customer has not asked for one.

## Scope and intent

This is a Synthetic Contoso demo procedure - fictional internal policy, not legal or regulatory authority.
Use this procedure when a customer appears to misunderstand credit, repayment, billing or BNPL terms and needs clarification rather than a sales conversation.

- Keep the interaction educational first.
- Provide plain-language clarification without steering the customer towards any credit product.

## Establish the customer’s understanding

Begin by asking the customer to explain, in their own words, what they think applies to their situation. Clarify terms only after you have understood their present belief.
Do not assume that a question about credit means the customer wants to take a product.

- Ask the customer what they believe, in their own words.
- Confirm whether the customer has asked for a credit product at all before any product discussion is considered.

## Verify the actual commitment

Where there is an existing BNPL or similar commitment, explain only from the actual terms available for that commitment. Pay particular attention to charges that may arise if the customer pays late or misses a due amount.
Do not state or imply that 'no cost' applies in every circumstance.

- Clarify that 'no cost' must be understood in the context of the stated terms and conditions.

## Provide education only

Explain core credit concepts in plain Indian English, using neutral wording and no sales language. Keep the explanation limited to helping the customer understand dues, charges and reporting effects.
If the customer has not asked for a product, do not discuss products.

- Where no product has been asked for, keep the interaction to education only, with no product discussed.

## Stopping point and escalation

If the customer has not asked for a product, stop at education and do not proceed into any product conversation. Record the interaction in line with internal practice, using factual and neutral wording.
If any authorisation beyond factual education is required, it must be referred onward for human judgement.

- Stop when the customer has not asked for a product.

## Required checks
- Ask the customer to state what they actually believe, in their own words.
- Check the real terms of any outstanding BNPL commitment, including late fees.
- Confirm whether the customer has asked for a credit product at all.

## Prohibited assumptions
- Do not assume that a customer asking about credit wants a card.
- Do not assume that 'no cost' means no cost in every circumstance.

## Stop conditions
- Stop if the customer has not asked for a product.

## Valid outcomes
- Explain minimum due, interest and bureau reporting plainly.
- Provide education only, with no product discussed.

## Human gate
