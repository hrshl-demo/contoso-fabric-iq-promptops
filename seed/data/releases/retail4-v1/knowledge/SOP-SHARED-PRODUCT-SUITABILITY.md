# Product suitability and comparison (SOP-SHARED-PRODUCT-SUITABILITY)

Synthetic Contoso demo procedure - fictional internal policy, not legal or regulatory authority.

**Applies to:** all cases   **Scope:** shared   **Source version:** 1

## Purpose
This procedure sets out how bank staff must conduct any product suitability review or comparison in a consistent, factual and customer-led manner, using only the customer’s stated need and the approved catalogue rules and figures.

## 1. Scope and principle

This is a Synthetic Contoso demo procedure - fictional internal policy, not legal or regulatory authority.
All product comparison activity must begin from the customer’s stated need and must remain factual, balanced and limited to what is supported by the approved catalogue.

- Do not begin a comparison by naming products before the customer’s need is recorded.
- Do not present a comparison as a contest that must end with a single winner.

## 2. Capture the customer need

Record the customer’s stated need in the customer’s own words before any product is named or discussed in comparison.
If the need is unclear, ask for clarification and document the response plainly.

- Use the customer’s own wording for the need statement.
- Keep the need statement separate from staff interpretation or sales language.

## 3. Verify eligibility and source data

Check each possible option only against the objective eligibility criteria stated in the approved catalogue.
Any figure quoted in the comparison must be taken from the approved catalogue source and not from narrative text, memory or informal material.

- Confirm objective eligibility against the catalogue’s stated rules.
- Use only approved catalogue figures in any comparison.
- Where evidence is required to support eligibility or the stated need, ensure it is available before proceeding.

## 4. Conduct the comparison

Where comparison is possible, present genuine alternatives that match the stated need and explain the trade-offs in clear, neutral language.
Staff must remain balanced and must not imply that the process is designed to produce one best or winning product.

- Describe differences factually, including relevant trade-offs.
- If appropriate, record that keeping the current arrangement is an outcome to be considered.
- If nothing suitable is available, record that no offer can be made.

## 5. When to stop and how to close

Stop the process immediately if the customer’s stated need is unknown or if required evidence is missing.
Only close the review with an allowed outcome that is supported by the recorded need, catalogue rules and available evidence.

- Do not continue a comparison when the stated need is unknown.
- Do not continue when required evidence is missing.
- Use only the permitted outcome statements for closure.

## Required checks
- Record the customer's stated need, in their words, before any product is named.
- Check objective eligibility against the catalogue's stated rules.
- Verify that every quoted figure comes from the approved catalogue, not from prose.

## Prohibited assumptions
- Do not assume a need from demographics, age, balance or cohort.
- Do not assume that a comparison must produce a winner.

## Stop conditions
- Stop if the stated need is unknown.
- Stop if required evidence is missing.

## Valid outcomes
- Two or three genuine alternatives with their trade-offs.
- Keep current.
- No offer, where nothing suits.

## Human gate
Only a human staff member may authorise the final wording of the comparison presented to the customer and the recorded outcome.
